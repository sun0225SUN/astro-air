When I was cutting in line, I fed pigs and herded cows. If no one is around, these two animals know exactly how to live. They will roam freely, eat and drink when they are hungry and thirsty, and talk about love when spring comes; in this way, their life level is very low and completely lackluster. After people came, they made arrangements for their lives: the lives of every cow and every pig had a theme. For most of them, this kind of life theme is very miserable: the theme of the former is to work, and the theme of the latter is to grow fat. I don’t think there is anything to complain about, because my life at that time was not much enriched. Apart from eight model plays, there was no entertainment. There are a very small number of pigs and cows, and their lives have other plans. Take pigs as an example. Breeding pigs and sows have other things to do besides eating. From what I've seen, they don't like these arrangements very much either. The breeding pig's job is to mate, in other words, our policy allows it to be a playboy. However, tired breeding pigs often adopt a gentlemanly attitude that is only reserved for meat pigs (porks are castrated) and refuse to jump on the back of the sow. The sow's job is to give birth to piglets, but some sows want to eat the piglets. In general, human arrangements make pigs miserable. But they still accepted it: pigs will always be pigs.

It is human nature to make various arrangements for life. Not only setting up the animals, but also setting up yourself. We know that there was Sparta in ancient Greece, where life was set up to be lifeless. The purpose was to make men into desperate warriors and women into fertility machines. The former was like a fighting cock, and the latter was like a sow. These two types of animals are very special, but I think they definitely don't like their lives. But what if you don’t like it? No matter whether people or animals, it is difficult for them to change their destiny.

The pig discussed below is somewhat unusual. When I fed the pig, it was already four or five years old. Technically speaking, it was a meat pig, but it was black and thin, with bright eyes. This guy is as agile as a goat and can jump over a one-meter-high pig pen; it can also jump on the roof of a pig pen, which is like a cat - so it always wanders around and doesn't stay in the pen at all. All educated youths who have fed pigs treat it as their favorite, and it is also my favorite - because it is only good to educated youths and allows them to come within three meters. If it had been anyone else, it would have run away long ago. It is male and should have been chopped off. But try it. Even if you hide the pig knife behind your back, it can smell it, stare at you with wide eyes, and roar. I always feed it porridge made from fine rice bran. After it has eaten enough, I put the bran into the weeds and feed it to other pigs. The other pigs saw jealousy and started shouting together. At this time, the whole pig farm was crying and howling, but neither I nor it cared. After eating, it jumps up to the roof to bask in the sun, or imitates various sounds. It can imitate the sound of cars and tractors, and it is very similar to them. Sometimes it disappears for the whole day. I guess it has gone to a nearby village to look for sows. We also have sows here, all kept in pens, out of shape from excessive reproduction, dirty and smelly, and they are not interested in them; the sows in the village are better-looking. It has many wonderful deeds, but I have only been feeding pigs for a short time and my knowledge is limited, so I simply won’t write about them. All in all, all educated youths who have fed pigs like it, like its unique style, and say that it lives a cool life. But the fellow villagers are not so romantic. They say that this pig is not serious. The leaders hate it, which will be discussed later. I not only like it - I respect it, and often call it "Brother Pig" regardless of the fact that I am more than ten years older. As mentioned before, this pig brother can imitate various sounds. I think it has also learned how to speak, but it has not learned it-if it had learned it, we could have a heart-to-heart talk. But you can't blame it. The timbres of humans and pigs are so different.

Later, Brother Pig learned how to whistle, but this skill brought him trouble. We have a sugar factory there, and the whistle is blown once at noon to let the workers change shifts. When our team was working in the fields, we heard the whistle and came back. My pig brother always jumps to the house to learn the whistle every morning at ten o'clock, and the people in the fields come back when they hear his whistle - this is an hour and a half before the sugar factory blows its whistle. Frankly speaking, Brother Pig cannot be entirely blamed for this. It is not a boiler after all, and its sound is somewhat different from that of a steam whistle, but the fellow villagers insist that they can't hear it. The leaders therefore held a meeting and determined that it was a bad element that was destroying spring plowing and that dictatorship measures were to be taken against it. I already knew the spirit of the meeting, but I was not worried about it. Because if dictatorship means ropes and butcher knives, there is no way. It’s not like the previous leaders haven’t tried, and even a hundred people couldn’t cure it. Dogs are useless: Brother Pig runs like a torpedo and can knock a dog ten feet away. Unexpectedly, this time he was serious about it. The instructor led more than 20 people, holding May 4 pistols; the deputy instructor led more than a dozen people, holding muskets for watching young people, and they divided into two groups to hunt for it in the open space outside the pig farm. This caused me to fall into an inner conflict: According to my friendship with it, I should rush out with two butcher knives and fight side by side with it, but I felt that doing so was too shocking - it is a pig after all; there was another reason, I did not dare to confront the leader, and I suspected that this was the problem. Anyway, I watched from the sidelines. I admire Brother Pig's calmness very much: he calmly hides within the line between the pistol and the musket, and does not leave that line no matter how people yell or the dog bites. In this way, if the person holding the pistol fires, the person holding the musket will be killed, and vice versa; if both ends fire at the same time, both ends will be killed. As for it, since the target is small, it's probably fine. After going around in circles for a few times, it found an opening and crashed out; running extremely gracefully. I saw it once again in the sugar cane field. It had grown fangs and recognized me, but it no longer allowed me to come near. This indifference pains me, but I also approve of the way it keeps people with ulterior motives at arm's length.

I am forty years old, and besides this pig, I have never seen anyone dare to ignore the settings of life so much. On the contrary, I have seen many people who want to set up other people's lives, and others who are content with their lives being set up. For this reason, I have always missed this maverick pig.
