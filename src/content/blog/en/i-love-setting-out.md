Every place you reach belongs to yesterday. No matter how green the mountains are, how beautiful the water is, and how gentle the wind is. Lingering too deeply becomes a kind of fetter, which not only blocks your feet, but also the future.

How could you not love setting off? It is a pity that we have never seen the majesty of the mountains; it is still a pity that we have seen the majesty of the mountains but have not seen the vastness of the sea; we are still regretful that we have seen the vastness of the sea but have not seen the vastness of the desert; we have seen the vastness of the desert but have not seen the mystery of the forest, which is still a pity. There are endless scenery in the world, and I have an eternal mood.

I naturally know that there are bumps in the mountains, waves in the sea, wind and sand in the desert, and wild beasts in the forest. Even so, I still like it.

Breaking the peace of life is another scene, a scene that belongs to youth. I'm so glad I'm not old yet. So what if you are really old? Isn't there a saying that old age makes you stronger?

So I want to learn profundity from the mountains, bravery from the sea, composure from the desert, and agility from the forest. I also want to learn to taste a colorful life.

How far can one go? This is not about feet but about ambition; how high can a person climb? This is not a matter of hands but of will.

Therefore, I want to use my passion to set a lofty goal for myself. Not only to strive for a kind of glory, but also to pursue a state. If the goal is achieved, it is glorious; if the goal is not achieved, life will become rich and fulfilling because of the ups and downs along the way; in my opinion, this is a life worth living.

## About the author

Wang Guozhen, born in Beijing on June 22, 1956, is a contemporary poet, calligrapher and painter. Graduated from the Chinese Department of Jinan University in 1982. In 1984, he published his first influential poem "I Smiled towards Life". Since 1985, he has focused his spare time on poetry creation. One of his limericks, "One Day at School," was published in China Youth Daily. Since 1990, Wang Guozhen has served as a columnist for "Liaoning Youth", "China Youth" and "Girlfriend", setting off a wave of "Wang Guozhen fever". Since 2005, his calligraphy works have been given to foreign political parties and national leaders as gifts for central leaders’ visits abroad; in the same year, he served as the director of the Literary and Art Creation Center of the Chinese Academy of Arts. Wang Guozhen died of illness at 2:10 a.m. on April 26, 2015, at the age of 59.
