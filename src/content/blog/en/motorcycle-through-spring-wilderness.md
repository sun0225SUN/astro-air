## The first volume of Altay texts (2007-2009)

Motorcycles are really a good thing because they are more powerful than us. In this desolate land, it can easily lead us to places beyond the reach of our feet. Of course, riding a motorcycle for a long time is also very tiring and not as easy as hiking. Many times I sit for five or six hours at a time, and by the time I get there, my legs are bowed. What's more, my motorcycle is just a small-sized motorcycle with a small fuel tank. Three people are always riding on it at the same time. The motorcycle is not comfortable, and the people are not comfortable either. Otherwise, there is nothing to be dissatisfied with.

I really wanted to learn to ride a motorcycle, but I was afraid of falling. I remember when I was a kid, I had almost no sense of balance and I didn’t even dare to swing on the swing. It took me three years to learn how to ride a bicycle, and two years to learn how to push a bicycle... In short, I thought that I would never dare to hope to use this kind of machine in my life. But I still like motorcycles, if they can whiz by in the wind - it seems that it is with such a tool that I can appear in the world more clearly and keenly. Otherwise... Well, actually, being limited by ability is not a bad thing. However, since we already have motorcycles, let’s just talk about motorcycles! ——When I stand on the earth and point with my hand: where I want to go! So I went. I suddenly feel uneasy about discovering that there may really be nothing impossible in this world anymore - as if we are using motorcycles to leave something behind impatiently, conveniently, quickly and completely forever... But, I also think that today, this is a reality that we cannot avoid and cannot refuse. Well, that's not a bad thing, right? Hey - when I stood on the ground and pointed with my hand: where I want to go!

Especially when we moved our family to Akhara on the edge of the desert, the use of motorcycles became even more important. By then we were well out of the mountains. Akhara is located in the Gobi Desert near the Ulungu River in the south, more than 200 kilometers away from the county seat. If you take a bus, a trip to the county seat in winter will cost fifty yuan. Even if it costs fifty yuan, you may not be able to get a ride. There is no official line bus in this village yet. Only some private black cars are in operation, most of them are eight-seater old jeeps, and there are only one or two of them a day at most. Often before dawn, the driver would go from one end of the village to the other to pick up people from door to door. Often the seats were full before we even got to our house. Or if there is an emergency, but the car is not full of people, it will not leave. It will stop at the entrance of the village and wait for a day or two, and there is nothing you can do about it. And motorcycles are so convenient, you can set off whenever you want. Moreover, if you ride a motorcycle to the county, the gas cost for the round trip is only a dozen yuan, which saves you eighty or ninety yuan! If two people go to the county, they can save 160; if three people go to the county, they can save 250. Tsk! Moreover, there is no need to get motion sickness.

But then again. The wind in the Gobi Desert is strong and the roads are difficult to travel. In order to save fuel, the speed of the motorcycle is controlled at a slower speed. So the wind always blows for four or five hours when I go out. It’s so frustrating. Although my mother got me a helmet, it was so heavy that it stuck to my head and made me dizzy. I couldn't wear it at all, so I had to hang it around my neck and let it hang on the back of my head. But when the wind blew, the helmet was pulled back hard by the full wind, causing the strap around the neck to get stuck in the flesh, making the person dizzy and half of his tongue sticking out. After a while, the front teeth were blown cold and dry. I had to take it off and hold it in my arms. But this way, there was a big gap between me and my uncle, and the wind blew in there. Even though he was wearing three layers of clothing, he was still blown through by the wind after a while. His arms were open and his belly felt cold. Despite wearing gloves, the fingers holding the helmet quickly became cold and hard, and I couldn't straighten them. Hey, I can’t wear it or not wear it. I don’t even have a place to put it. There’s really nothing I can do with this thing...

The road we took was a dirt road in the Gobi Desert (--it's so shameful, my uncle still doesn't have a license and doesn't dare to go on the highway...). It's not so much a road as it is the trace of a thin and faint road, bumpy and undulating. The road seemed to be on the verge of being abandoned and we could hardly see another car walking this way for hours. The earth is vast and turbulent, and the sky is even wider—the sky accounts for three-quarters of the world, and the earth accounts for one-quarter.

The world in front of you is clear and unobstructed. In our field of vision, there are three whirlwinds. Among them, the one directly in front of us is the tallest, twenty to thirty meters high, tilting and swaying from side to side, like a pillar holding between the sky and the earth. There are two wind pillars on our left, located one kilometer away above a snow-white, grassless salt-alkali flat. Therefore, the two wind pillars are also snow-white. And the sky is so blue... This is late spring in May, but in the northern continent where winter lasts for half a year, this time is just the beginning of spring. The color of the grass looks far away but there is no grass up close. The ground at our feet is rough and dull. But in the distance all the way to the horizon, there is already a scene of blue fields. The snow-white salt-alkali shoals on the earth are continuously distributed on the left and right, surrounded by clusters of grass, white, green, colorful and open. Later, I saw that the two snow-white cyclones on the left gradually merged into one, and the one not far ahead of our road was gradually receding and extinguished.

We run on the earth from north to south, and the wind blows from west to east on the earth. My hair also flew straight in the wind. The wind "pressed" hard on my face. I thought my face had been pressed very hard. It would be nice if you had a big headscarf with you at this time. Covering it from head to toe like Uighur women, you will definitely be invulnerable. So I had to put the helmet on my head to block the wind. But after a while, I couldn't breathe smoothly and felt very suffocated. I had to take it off again, but as soon as I took it off, I immediately realized the benefits of wearing it, so I put it back on again with a lot of shaking. Immediately I couldn't breathe again...

Because it was a bargain sold in my own store, the plastic windshield of this broken helmet had long been worn away by wind and sand. The world I saw was dirty and hazy, and everything I could see was a mess. After a long time, I felt nauseous and dizzy. I had to close my eyes...it was really too heavy! I don’t know if it’s really heavy, or if my senses have been sensitively acting on that place for a long time and I feel “heavy” in a different way. Anyway, it was so heavy that I was hunched over all the way.

Such wind! It gallops from the far end of the horizon, and the sky and the earth are filled with moans. Compared with this huge, powerful voice, my personal voice has become something like a "breath", as weak and inaccurate as the words spoken in a dream. The wind was so strong that it was impossible for me to maintain a calmer expression along the way. Really, several times, I would suddenly realize that I was frowning and grinning.

While taking a break, I looked in the rearview mirror of the car and was shocked to find that I was missing two front teeth! Taking a closer look, it turned out that the front teeth had turned black... They were all blown by the wind and covered with mud. The lips were also black, stiff and chapped. This season is when the dust is blowing wildly. The windshield of my uncle's helmet was also covered with a thick layer of dust. It's hard to imagine how he persisted until now and could still drive correctly in the middle of the dirt road. I wiped it with my palms, but the more I wiped it, the dirtier it became. I had to use my sleeves instead.

We stood by the car to rest, terribly thirsty. The wind howled between heaven and earth, my hair was disheveled and my facial muscles were stiff. The wind was so strong - later I accidentally dropped five dollars in such a wind, and chased after the money for hundreds of meters without being able to catch up. Fortunately, I was finally caught by a clump of splendens.

I paid for gasoline, and I bought gasoline because we ran out of oil. The reason for the lack of oil was because the oil tank was leaking, and a pipe inserted into the oil tank fell off somehow... Anchorage in the Gobi Desert is something that must be welcomed and faced calmly at any time. Because that's a "just in case" thing. Therefore, my uncle was still cheerful and did not feel guilty at all for causing me harm due to his negligence.

He just smiled and told me that there was another time that was even worse. Halfway through, he had a flat tire. So, that time he pushed the car for nine hours in the Gobi Desert... If he had to ask me to accompany him for another nine hours this time, I swear, I would never go out as soon as I got home. It was too dangerous to go out.

The surrounding fields are completely empty, with not even a tree in sight. Where can I find gasoline?

We are very lucky. When I usually take this road, except for an occasional shepherd or two, I won't see a single ghost from beginning to end. But this time the car broke down, and within a short while, another motorcycle came over from the end of the field of vision, carrying billows of dust. We waved to him from afar. As he got closer, he saw a young man who worked in the animal husbandry industry. His face was dark and red, and his eyes were sharp and bright. We gestured to let him understand our situation, and he immediately went to unscrew the cap of his oil tank. I quickly found a container to catch the oil. But after rummaging through the backpack for a long time, I only found a small "Wahaha" yogurt bottle used for sewing and sewing. So the two men overturned the motorcycle, and I carefully held the too small and slender bottle at the clear stream flowing out of the fuel tank. However, once I connected five or six bottles, I was no longer embarrassed to ask for them. I am also traveling far away. What if something happens and I run out of gas? Finally, in order to express my gratitude, I wanted to give him some money, so...

The two of them stood in the wind, watching me chase the note farther and farther away. Like it will never come back.

Later, when I gave him the money, he thanked us profusely and was extremely grateful to us.

We continued to run insignificantly on the Gobi Desert, with the dust behind us. It was getting dark. The dirt road also became looming and intermittent. It's not this way, we're on the wrong track, we're lost.

Getting lost in the Gobi Desert is a terrible thing. At night the temperature will drop to about zero degrees and the wind may become stronger. Moreover, once you are really lost, the more anxious you are, the more confused you will be about your direction. This land is vast and seems to extend in all directions, but in fact every step may lead to a place where you can never return to the previous step.

We entered an ocher-red undulating area that sunk into the earth, but before that, we had no impression of this place at all. I reminded my uncle to go back, but he thought that we were heading south anyway, and no matter how we walked, we would reach the Ulungu River. If we walked downstream along the Wu River, we would reach home no matter how we walked. I didn't have any opinion, so I had to listen to him.

In the west of the earth, there are quiet horses moving slowly under the setting sun, dragging long shadows. A young horse herder, with his long whip hanging down, sat quietly on the horse, looking towards us for a long time. I suggested asking the kid for directions, but he was too far away. And my uncle wanted to walk a few more kilometers, get out of the red Gobi Desert, and walk to the high ground ahead to look at the terrain.

Later, we still had to go back and find the boy. But we left the light-colored dirt road and drove into the dark, naked and rough wilderness. We walked northwest for a long time, and we could no longer find the horses and children. Probably lost again. The earth is empty and unobstructed, and the clouds in the sky are getting denser. Although the world remains clear, dusk has really come. Those five or six small bottles of gasoline have been burned to this day, and I don’t know how long they can continue to burn.

We stopped on the Gobi Desert, with the hardened ground covered with sparse hay under our feet. I bent down and dug out a small pebble from the soil crust at my feet. After wiping it clean, I discovered that it was a light yellow transparent agate with reddish blood oozing out. Looking around again, there are beautiful stones like this everywhere under my feet, one by one tightly embedded in the hard ground. I picked up a bunch of them randomly and put them in my pocket. At this time, I raised my head and saw smoke and dust rising in the distance.

We quickly got on the car and chased to that place, and gradually we saw clearly that it was actually a truck - or the light blue "Old Jiefang" with a large protrusion from the front... What a ghost. It’s as if we’ve returned to the past when we lost our way.

It was only when I got closer that I saw clearly that the car was really broken, rattling and swaying on the ground, and could fall apart at any time. It must be a black car, and it can only travel on such a "dark road" for the rest of its life. It has been hidden from a long time ago until now, like a lost thing carefully preserved for the world...

When the driver noticed someone was chasing him, he stopped and stood still on the ground in the distance. When we arrived, he was leaning on the half-open car door rolling a Mohe cigarette.

He suggested that we follow his car, otherwise it would be too dangerous. But the place he wanted to go was not the same as the place we wanted to go. Although we could go back from there, we were really in a hurry to get home and didn't want to go any further. Moreover, wherever the cart goes, dust is everywhere, and it is not comfortable to eat dirt behind it. So after carefully asking for directions, I thanked him and said goodbye.

The driver repeatedly warned us not to take the fork in the west. Whenever we encounter a fork in the road, we must remember to turn left. If we keep turning left, we will reach Wuhe River.

This driver is such a nice guy, just like his antique car. He also took out a pipe, poured some more oil into our gas tank, and finally gave us half a bottle of water.

We moved in two directions, gradually walking farther and farther away from each other on the earth.

I alternately put both hands in my coat pockets to keep warm, and one hand pinched my uncle's shoulder tightly. He drove faster and faster, and the wind became stronger and stronger. But I was thinking: I will never go back to a place with agate again...
